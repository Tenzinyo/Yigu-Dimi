Okay so where do I start.. 

Nowadays, we are constantly on our phones and watching all sorts of media catered “carefully” towards our tastes and what we might “want” next. We are living in a time where doom scrolling is more normal than wanting to take a walk outside with your friend. What in our biochemistry that happens in our brain tells us that okay we have had enough of the hour or the day, that we deserve to take out some time to be on our phones and snooze outside of reality while another reality is shown to us in vibrant, pleasing ways. If the instant gratification of wanting to snooze out of reality is through short 15 second videos, how much does it really take to make us get out of touch with reality. 

Does our need to be in our own space in the comfort of our devices, stem from the pressure we feel in existing within our communities, if so growing up in early 2010s, with social media being more inaccessible and irrelevant to one's lived experience, why did we not feel the need to be online? 

How much does being online really control how we feel about so many things. From the GPS that we use to navigate our daily lives, to the watch from the ad that glorifies my checkout bucket full of kitchenware, to the movie recommendations that we do not necessarily finish, is linked to cater our wants, in this materialistic and proconsumption world where everybody wants the next buzz. Who really controls that buzz? 

Okay lets swerve towards the AI debate? In the beginning of 2023, there were mixed reactions and acceptance towards AI chatbots, whether they were groundbreaking technology that will make our work more efficient or just an excuse to finish up my paper, email or fix that broken code with so many bugs. In academia or corporate America, Professors or Managers weren't as pro-AI as they are today. As a college student, I went from feeling guilty in using AI to finish my project to feeling embarrassed I am not using it. But we all agree on one thing, despite AI sucking out a whole ocean we believe that it is much needed for innovation in issues of accessibility, improving research on health and medicine for diseases which are not only hard to pronounce but also incurable, and many more except for making an AI avatar of a mystical world where elephants are flying and selling it online. 

What happened in between, that most institutions and corporations became pro AI to the point that it started taking so much jobs, because AI was automating so many inefficiencies in a system that we really didn't need people to work on that, I know it's definitely not to cover up on the exorbitant costs with subscribing to that chatbot. 

And just like that, the job market is flooded with so many new grads as well as people like President Obama competing for the same resources? How can the new grad make it when they are competing with not only AI but Senior Devs with an experience worth their whole life, while they are questioning why they were sucking on that pacifier when they should have been coding to meet the reality of today's job market? 

And maybe that's why we are doom scrolling because suddenly the reality feels so complicated and unfathomable. Might as well just learn some new slangs online. At least we will find ourselves relating to those memes more than whatever Elon Musk is trying to do.

Now it brings me to the question, are we becoming irrelevant to the very system we created ourselves?
